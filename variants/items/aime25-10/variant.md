# aime25-10 variant

The $18$ cells of a $3 \times 6$ grid are filled in using the numbers $1$ through $6$ so that each row contains $6$ different numbers, and each of the three $3 \times 2$ blocks heavily outlined in the example below contains $6$ different numbers.

\[
\begin{array}{|c|c||c|c||c|c|}
\hline
3 & 5 & 1 & 6 & 2 & 4 \\
\hline
1 & 6 & 2 & 4 & 3 & 5 \\
\hline
2 & 4 & 3 & 5 & 6 & 1 \\
\hline
\end{array}
\]

The number of different ways to fill such a grid can be written as $p^a \cdot q^b \cdot r^c$, where $p$, $q$, $r$ are distinct prime numbers and $a$, $b$, $c$ are positive integers. Find $p \cdot a + q \cdot b + r \cdot c$.

**Answer: 38** (count = 460800 = 2^11 · 3^2 · 5^2)
